# City Thief: mission research (v7)

What the research says about great missions, and how City Thief uses it.
Sources are listed at the end.

## 1. What a mission is made of

- A 2026 study coded hundreds of open-world missions (including GTA V) as chains of "action blocks":
  move, fight, sneak, drive, talk, solve. Main missions average about 16 blocks, side missions about 12.
- Fighting and moving are the base of almost every mission. **Puzzles and problem-solving are almost always missing.**
  That is a gap we can use: small puzzles (find the code, pick the right person, choose a route).
- Variety rule from practising designers: every mission type should have **3–5 variations**
  (for example a chase on foot, by car, by boat, in traffic, at night).

**City Thief rule:** every mission mixes 3+ different blocks, and every chapter has at least one light puzzle.

## 2. Teach, grow, twist, finish (Nintendo's 4 steps)

- Nintendo builds Mario levels in four steps (kishōtenketsu): **introduce** a mechanic safely, **develop** it,
  **twist** it (a new context), then **conclude**.
- The twist is not always a plot shock. Often it is the *same* skill used in a new way.

**City Thief rule:** each chapter introduces 2–3 new "verbs" (pickpocket, disguise, photo, passengers...).
The first mission teaches the verb with no risk, the next ones raise the stakes, a late mission twists it,
and the chapter boss uses all of them.

## 3. Pacing: peaks and rests

- Pacing is the ratio of intensity to rest. Good missions climb in intensity **with small rests**.
  Climbing in a straight line tires players; doing the same thing again bores them.
- Plan the intensity of each mission and of the whole chapter in advance (not "on the fly").

**City Thief rule:** each mission has a calm start (30 s), a twist in the middle and a loud finish.
Chapters alternate loud missions (chases, fights) with quiet ones (stealth, talking, driving jobs).

## 4. Choice and the heist structure (GTA V)

- GTA V heists split into **setup missions** (get tools, cars, intel) and a **finale**.
  The player picks an **approach** and a **crew**, and the approach decides which setups you play.
- Time spent on setups makes the finale feel earned.

**City Thief rule:** the last chapter is one big heist: setups first, then a finale with a choice
(quiet disguise or loud assault).

## 5. Hiding in a crowd (Hitman "social stealth")

- Hitman's sneaking is about blending into crowds, not shadows. Wearing a disguise is not enough:
  **you also have to act the part.** Crowds become a cat-and-mouse escape.
- Familiar public places (a market, a wedding, a rally) teach players the social rules without a tutorial.

**City Thief rule:** disguises (delivery, police, worker, office) work until you run, aim or stay too close;
a suspicion meter shows the danger. Indian public spaces (bazaar, wedding, rally, festival) are the levels.

## 6. People who feel alive (Red Dead Redemption 2, Watch Dogs 2)

- RDR2: strangers give small missions; random street events; you can greet or antagonise people and they react.
- Watch Dogs 2: scanning a person shows a **profile** (name, job, a funny fact). This made crowds feel human,
  and gave missions like "find the right person in a crowd".

**City Thief rule:** every person can be talked to, robbed, threatened, pushed, helped or hired.
Talking shows a profile card with a name, job and a funny fact. Witnesses call the police.

## 7. Mobile sessions and rewards

- Mobile session length: medians from about **5–7 minutes** (GameAnalytics 2025) to about 18 minutes (InvestGame 2026).
- Loops: core action loop 10–90 s, progression loop 5–30 min, meta loop over weeks.
- Players prefer **variable** reward schedules over fixed ones (Princeton and MIT studies).
- India: about 95% of 500 million players are on mobile; battle royale is the top genre (Niko Partners).

**City Thief rule:** missions last 2–5 minutes; something rewarding happens every 10–20 s;
random bonus cash and rare drops; daily reward and rank keep the long loop.

## 8. Ideas taken from classic mission lists

- GTA San Andreas: chases, escorts, burglary, photography, taxi, paramedic, freight delivery.
- Sleeping Dogs and Yakuza: side cases with chains of leads, karaoke and fight clubs, surreal small stories.
- Open-world "best side content" lists: activities people love are short, replayable and show off the city.
